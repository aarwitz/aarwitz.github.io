Fine-Tuning π0 on My Sock-Collecting Robot

Jul 24, 2026 • Aaron

  • I have been using a CNN-detection and vSLAM stack with a differential driver PI controller on MobileRobot.
  • Grasping arbitrary objects is the hard part, so I finally put a real robot foundation model in the loop.
  • π0, LoRA fine-tuned on 50 demonstrations, 11 minutes of robot time, and $4 of rented GPU, picked a sock off a box in my living room in 2 of 3 live attempts.
  • The model saw one RGB camera, four floats of proprioception, and the prompt: "pick up the sock."
  • Every failure I saw traced back to a specific, fixable property of my data, not to the model.

Teleop first

  • I do teleop from a web page with WASD keys, since I do not have a VR rig or a space mouse.
  • The arm's vendor protocol was the interesting part: the documented "move to XYZ" command (T:104) is blocking and can wedge the ESP32 if streamed at 10 Hz.
  • T:123 is a real velocity-jog command, and T:1041 is a non-blocking setpoint.
  • Those two commands, plus server-side acceleration limiting, made motion smooth enough to look intentional.

The grasp had to be engineered before it could be demonstrated

  • The RoArm's gripper only moves one jaw; the other is a rigid backstop.
  • Pinching a scrunched sock from above fails because the claw closes on a fold and the sock extrudes back out as the arm lifts.
  • What works is a sweep: descend beside the sock on the moving-jaw side, fold the last 25 mm into a lateral sweep, and close during the sweep so the moving jaw drags the sock into the fixed jaw.
  • The gripper servo's torque reading is my force sensor: nothing settles around 32, while a sock strains at 150–240.
  • Every demonstration gets verified twice, after the grab and again after the lift.

11 minutes of data, one rented A40

  • The collector drives the arm purely over ROS topics, so the browser E-STOP halts scripted collection too.
  • Ground truth chains forward with no perception in the loop: wherever the sock is placed becomes the next episode's pick point, with bounded random jitter.
  • I collected 50 clean episodes at about 13 seconds each, for 6,808 frames.
  • Each frame carries RGB, cartesian pose, joint angles, and all four servo torques at 15 Hz.
  • I converted to LeRobot format with cartesian actions, padded the 4-DoF state into π0's 32-dim action space, masked the wrist cameras I do not have, and converted absolute setpoints to delta actions.
  • Training was LoRA on the pi0_base checkpoint, 5,000 steps at 6.1 s/step on a RunPod A40 at $0.44/hr, or about $4 total.

What the policy actually gets

  • Per inference, the policy sees one 224×224 letterboxed frame, four floats of real arm state, and the prompt.
  • No depth, no detector, and no history are used.
  • It returns a 50×4 action chunk, which is about 3.3 seconds of motion at the demo rate.
  • Each action becomes a setpoint on the same topic my keyboard uses, with envelope clamping and E-STOP gating.
  • I recorded a full run to a rosbag: 601 commands, 15 inferences, and 5,038 camera frames.

Did it work?

  • A dry run with the motors off reproduced the trained sweep almost exactly.
  • That was the moment I knew the pipeline was real.
  • Live, I got two clean picks out of three attempts, verified by the same torque telemetry the collector uses.
  • One run picked and placed repeatedly.
  • The best one grasped, held through the lift, then released 40 mm above the surface, re-descended, and froze.
  • The freeze is behavior-cloning mode averaging made visible by the action-space choice: x, y, z as delta actions freeze when the model is unsure, while the absolute gripper oscillates between open and close values.

Five things I learned

  • Pretrained generality is real and cheap to tap. Eleven minutes of demos on an embodiment π0 never saw still produced a working visuomotor pick.
  • Zero-shot cross-embodiment does not work; fine-tuning bridges it. The stock DROID checkpoint cannot drive this arm at all, but the gap closed with a config file.
  • Never curate away your failures. If the policy never sees recovery states, it has no mode to express when things go wrong.
  • Respect the chunk. Executing the full temporal structure of the data was key; mid-chunk replans injected jitter the policy never saw.
  • Verify state; never trust your own success message. Explicit outcome markers matter on a robot because software hygiene is safety.

Round two

  • π0's recipe is two layers: a base model with roughly 10,000 hours of cross-robot teleop, then a fine-tune with 1–20 hours of your task.
  • My 11 minutes sits far below that floor and still picked, which is the strongest evidence I have for what the base layer is worth.
  • Next up is 200–300 episodes, keeping miss-and-retry sequences, position variety, a few sock types, and both lighting conditions.
  • Same pipeline, another roughly $4 to retrain.
  • My prediction is that the freeze disappears, place height tightens, and success becomes repeatable.
